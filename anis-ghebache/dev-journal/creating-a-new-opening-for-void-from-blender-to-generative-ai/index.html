Creating a New Opening for Void: From Blender to Generative AI

By Anis Ghebache

Today, I experimented with creating an entirely new introductory shot for Void, combining 3D animation, generative AI and traditional compositing techniques.

The objective was to introduce a hauntingly beautiful atmosphere before transitioning into the opening scene envisioned by Liza, the film's director.

What began as an exploration of pacing quickly developed into one of my favourite visual experiments so far.

Reimagining the Introduction

In her production notes, Liza described a particular opening shot in which the camera stalks a character from behind a pillar. As the handheld camera moves, the character gradually emerges from the blur, almost as though materialising into existence.

It's a beautiful shot, with a mysterious quality that complements the film's existential atmosphere.

However, one element bothered me. A bright light behind the character gradually becomes a recognisable rectangular studio light as the camera moves. I felt that this unintentionally revealed the constructed nature of the set, diminishing some of the ambiguity of the scene.

Removing the light digitally would also be difficult, particularly because of its position behind the character's head and the way it illuminates the environment.

Rather than attempting to remove it, I decided to explore a different approach.

I wanted to introduce an additional shot that would give this light a new visual association, using a match cut between the moon and the original opening.

A Moonlit View of London

The inspiration came from Hampstead Heath, one of my favourite places in London.

From its elevated viewpoints, you can see the city's skyline framed by trees and an expansive night sky. I wanted to recreate that atmosphere, partly because of its beauty, but also because it would subtly reinforce the film's identity as a London production.

I imagined an opening in which the camera moves past a tree trunk, revealing the skyline beneath a luminous moon.

There would be something serene about the image, but also slightly uncanny.

As the camera moves, the moon gradually rises towards the upper edge of the frame, eventually aligning with the bright light in Liza's existing shot.

The resulting match cut would transition from a peaceful outdoor environment into the mysterious interior of Void, followed by an abrupt cut to a wide shot of the room.

I particularly liked this emotional progression: serenity, visual continuity, and then sudden disorientation.

Building the Scene in Blender

I began constructing the environment in Blender using assets from BlenderKit.

I sourced a tree trunk and a separate tree, removing the latter's trunk so I could arrange its foliage independently. This allowed me to establish a composition with a strong foreground silhouette, leaves framing the upper portion of the image, and the London skyline in the distance.

For the skyline, I used a reference image found online and applied it to a plane.

I configured a Damped Track constraint so that the plane's orientation followed the camera without translating through the scene. Given the apparent distance of the skyline, this was a practical way to maintain the illusion of a distant background.

Rather than moving the camera around the foreground tree, I animated the tree trunk itself so that it would obscure the camera in a manner resembling the pillar movement in the original footage.

I also used the Camera Shakify add-on, applying its Wedding preset to introduce subtle handheld movement and reproduce the natural instability of the film's cinematography.

For the moon, I applied a lunar texture to a sphere and used a Fresnel-based effect to create a luminous outline around its edges.

My intention wasn't to produce a photorealistic render entirely within Blender. Instead, I wanted to establish sufficiently accurate geometry, lighting, textures and camera movements for generative AI to interpret.

Using AI to Refine the Environment

Once the composition was established, I used ChatGPT to develop a more realistic interpretation of the rendered scene.

One interesting detail involved the foliage. I hadn't found a suitable free oak tree asset through BlenderKit, so I'd used foliage from another tree instead.

During the image-generation stage, I asked ChatGPT to replace those leaves with oak leaves to better complement the trunk and create a more coherent environment.

I also refined the moon's appearance, making its glow more atmospheric while preserving the composition and lighting established in Blender.

This approach allowed me to retain control over the scene's fundamental structure while using generative AI to improve its visual fidelity.

From Animation to Generative Video

The next stage involved BytePlus Canvas, where I experimented with using the Blender animation as a motion reference and the enhanced image as a visual reference.

I prepared a short animation intended to last approximately four to five seconds, considering both the desired duration of the opening and the platform's input requirements. I also wanted to be mindful of generation credits.

Interestingly, the greatest challenge wasn't constructing the environment or animating the camera. These were techniques I was already comfortable using.

Instead, it was communicating the intended relationship between the inputs to the generative model.

During my initial attempts, the system effectively reproduced the Blender animation without applying the visual transformation I wanted.

I realised that the terminology and ordering of my instructions were particularly important. The animation needed to provide the movement and composition, while the enhanced image needed to guide the final visual appearance.

Once I refined this workflow, I was able to produce a much more convincing result.

It was an interesting reminder that working with generative AI increasingly involves understanding how to communicate creative intentions precisely, rather than simply supplying a prompt and hoping for the best.

Compositing and Final Adjustments

I brought the generated footage into Adobe Premiere Pro for further refinement.

Here, I spent time adjusting the colour grade, introducing lens blur and working with masks to better integrate the generated sequence with the original footage.

The process also gave me an opportunity to explore Premiere Pro's newer masking and tracking tools.

Having primarily worked with Premiere Pro 2024 and earlier versions, I found the updated workflow particularly enjoyable, especially the improvements to tracking.

My aim was to ensure that the generated footage didn't feel disconnected from the live-action cinematography.

The handheld movement, shifting focus and eventual transition into the original scene all needed to feel like parts of the same visual language.

Finding Beauty in the Transition

What I'm particularly pleased with is how naturally the new shot transitions into Liza's original opening.

The moon rises towards the upper edge of the frame, creating a visual correspondence with the light positioned behind the character in the next shot.

The foreground tree also mirrors the pillar that partially conceals the character, maintaining a sense of continuity between the two environments.

The transition feels satisfying, almost dreamlike, before the film abruptly cuts to a wide shot of the interior.

That contrast is exactly what I wanted to achieve.

The opening begins with a serene, moonlit landscape, gradually introduces something uncanny, and then disrupts that tranquillity.

To me, it captures the haunting beauty and existential absurdism that make Void so compelling.

Respecting the Director's Vision

An important consideration throughout this experiment was maintaining Liza's original artistic intentions.

When I shared the concept with her, she reminded me to refer to her production notes regarding the opening scene.

I haven't received specific feedback on the new sequence yet, so I don't want to presume how she feels about it.

However, my intention was never to replace the opening she'd envisioned.

The stalking camera movement, the pillar and the gradual reveal of the character remain intact. What I've proposed is an additional shot that precedes that sequence, establishing an atmosphere and introducing visual symbolism that carries into her original composition.

I enjoy bringing new ideas into collaborative projects, but I also recognise the importance of respecting the director's creative authority.

Ultimately, the aim is to complement her vision, not compete with it.

Reflections on the Experiment

Regardless of whether this sequence makes it into the final film, I already consider the experiment a success.

I'm genuinely delighted with the result.

I love the composition, the atmosphere and the way the moonlit landscape transitions into the original footage. More importantly, I feel that the sequence achieves the emotional effect I intended.

From a technical perspective, it was also a satisfying opportunity to combine skills I've developed independently.

Blender gave me precise control over the composition, lighting and movement. ChatGPT helped refine the visual appearance, while BytePlus allowed me to explore transforming that animation into more realistic footage. Premiere Pro then provided the tools to bring everything together.

What's particularly exciting to me is how these different technologies can complement one another.

Rather than relying on generative AI to make every creative decision, I was able to establish the scene myself and use AI to develop its visual qualities.

The result is something that feels much closer to my original intention than I might have achieved through prompting alone.

For me, that's where the creative potential of these emerging tools becomes particularly interesting.

The technology isn't replacing the artistic process. It's becoming another means of expressing it.
