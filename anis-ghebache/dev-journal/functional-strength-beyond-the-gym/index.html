Functional Strength Beyond the Gym

By Anis Ghebache

An Unexpected Day of Physical Activity

I spent Sunday helping my sister move house, which involved several hours of carrying furniture, transporting boxes and navigating rather awkward spaces.

We began by moving everything downstairs, fitting objects into a relatively small lift, and loading them into a van before transporting them across London. We then had to unload everything and carry it up another series of stairs.

Although many of the boxes were relatively uniform, several of the larger objects were irregularly shaped, with uneven weight distribution and limited opportunities to establish a comfortable grip.

This required a considerable amount of repositioning, coordination and weight shifting.

Interestingly, despite the physical demands of the day, I didn't find the work particularly difficult. The objects were heavy, certainly, but I felt capable of moving them without experiencing the level of fatigue I might previously have expected.

Yoga and Muscular Endurance

One thing the experience illuminated was the apparent improvement in my physical endurance.

I've made several changes to my routine over recent months, including dietary improvements, supplementation and more consistent yoga practice.

It's difficult to determine which of these changes, if any, contributed most significantly to my perceived improvement. However, I suspect yoga has played an important role.

Many yoga sessions require me to sustain relatively demanding positions for extended periods, often while maintaining balance, controlling my breathing and resisting the temptation to release a posture prematurely.

Carrying heavy furniture isn't entirely dissimilar in this respect. There are moments when the challenge isn't simply generating enough force to move an object, but sustaining that force while maintaining an awkward position.

Of course, yoga postures and carrying furniture involve different loads and mechanical demands, so I wouldn't necessarily assume that improvements in one directly translate to the other.

Nevertheless, the similarities were interesting enough to make me reconsider how the different elements of my training might complement one another.

The Importance of Planning Movement

There were also several moments when the physical difficulty of the move was compounded by the way we organised ourselves.

At one point, it was suggested that the older men, including my father, should remain inside the van to receive and reposition the furniture.

I disagreed with this arrangement because the limited height of the van would require them to remain bent over while handling relatively heavy objects.

Later, we encountered another situation in which several smaller items were positioned behind much larger pieces of furniture.

My father wanted to retrieve the smaller objects first, despite the restricted access. I suggested waiting until we had enough people available to move the larger objects out of the way.

Unfortunately, he attempted to manoeuvre around them, twisting and contorting his torso while handling the furniture. He subsequently experienced some soreness in his back, although he was fine afterwards.

The experience reinforced something fairly simple: planning a movement can be just as important as having the physical strength to complete it.

Rather than treating every obstacle as a challenge to overcome through additional effort, it can be considerably more efficient to reorganise the environment and simplify the movement itself.

Reconsidering Functional Training

Throughout the day, I found myself thinking about the Indian clubs I've been using for shoulder exercises, alongside the possibility of introducing farmer's carries into my routine.

Much of my conventional resistance training involves relatively controlled movements, where the equipment follows a predictable path and the load is distributed in a familiar manner.

Moving furniture introduces considerably more variability.

The centre of mass can shift, the available grip might be uncomfortable, and the environment can require you to adopt positions that would rarely occur during a conventional gym exercise.

I'm interested in whether incorporating more loaded carries and multiplanar movements could improve my ability to manage these situations.

Reflections

Sunday was an interesting reminder that physical strength exists beyond the relatively controlled environment of the gym.

I was pleased with my endurance and my ability to sustain demanding movements throughout the day.

More importantly, the experience encouraged me to reconsider how I might incorporate functional strength into my existing routine.

I don't necessarily think that conventional resistance training is inadequate. Rather, I'm interested in exploring how different forms of physical practice might complement one another, particularly when confronted with movements that are difficult to anticipate or reproduce in isolation.
