Developing Cartoon Absurdity: The Dyslexic Anti-Fascist Society

By Anis Ghebache

Exploring a More Expressive Visual Language

Following my initial idea of producing handmade T-shirts inspired by Burlap Friends, I began experimenting with illustrations that could extend the visual identity of the original film.

Rather than simply reproducing existing character designs, I wanted to explore more elaborate compositions featuring exaggerated expressions, physical comedy and absurd situations.

I was particularly interested in the expressive animation styles associated with Looney Tunes, Courage the Cowardly Dog and Pizza Tower.

These works demonstrate how relatively simple characters can become extraordinarily dynamic through distortion, exaggerated movement and extreme facial expressions.

I wanted to explore a similar approach through a static illustration.

The challenge was to communicate the energy of an animated sequence within a single image.

The Dyslexic Anti-Fascist Society

The illustration developed around a deliberately absurd misunderstanding.

An elderly character is reading a book about Minecraft with his grandson.

However, another character misreads the title as Mein Kampf and responds with outrage.

Above the scene sits a banner reading "Dyslexic Anti-Fascist Society", providing the context for the misunderstanding.

The central joke is the wildly disproportionate reaction to an entirely innocent situation.

I wanted the scene to be ridiculous, exaggerated and slightly provocative.

Rather than presenting violence realistically, I was interested in the conventions of slapstick animation, where characters can behave with absurd aggression and their physical expressions become exaggerated beyond plausibility.

Constructing the Scene

The composition features several characters responding to the unfolding situation in dramatically different ways.

The elderly character is being held up by an angry woman who is preparing to deliver an enormous punch.

Her body is angled downwards, with her arm drawn back in an exaggerated wind-up that occupies a significant portion of the composition.

I wanted the pose itself to communicate an overwhelming sense of anger.

The movement needed to feel so excessive that the viewer could almost anticipate the force of the impending action.

Elsewhere, another character is jumping in place, visibly horrified by the situation.

This contrast between the characters' reactions helps establish a sense of comic chaos.

Their exaggerated expressions are intended to provide much of the image's energy, allowing the scene to communicate through gestures rather than relying entirely on written explanation.

Using Thought Bubbles as a Storytelling Device

One recurring visual device within Burlap Friends is the use of thought bubbles to communicate the characters' internal experiences.

I decided to incorporate this into the illustration.

As the elderly character is being threatened, he imagines himself standing outside a simple Minecraft house on a sunny day, resting his hand affectionately on his grandson's head.

The imagined environment contrasts dramatically with the violence occurring in the main composition.

However, even within this peaceful fantasy, the grandson is reacting with abject fear to the threat against his grandfather.

Meanwhile, in the actual scene, the grandson remains distracted by a crude Minecraft construction resembling a penis.

This was another deliberately vulgar joke, introducing an additional layer of absurdity.

I wasn't entirely certain whether the gag contributed meaningfully to the composition or simply made the image unnecessarily chaotic, but I found the contrast amusing.

I particularly liked the idea that the characters could occupy the same environment while responding to entirely different concerns.

The Challenge of Visual Clarity

Although I completed the illustration, I encountered some difficulties communicating the central joke.

The most significant issue concerned the book itself.

Because the elderly character is physically holding the book, its positioning is restricted by his anatomy and the overall composition.

I made the book relatively large to ensure that its title remained visible.

However, the angle at which the character holds it means that the lettering is also presented sideways.

This makes the title more difficult to read, particularly when viewing the image quickly.

Given that the joke depends on the distinction between Minecraft and Mein Kampf, this creates a potential problem.

If the viewer cannot immediately recognise the title, the woman's exaggerated reaction may appear completely arbitrary.

The banner provides some additional context, but I'm not entirely convinced that it communicates the misunderstanding independently.

This raised an interesting question about the relationship between visual complexity and effective storytelling.

Although the composition contains several humorous details, those details risk competing for the viewer's attention.

An illustration can be energetic and technically successful while still struggling to communicate its intended narrative clearly.

Considering the Design as Clothing

Another consideration was whether the illustration would function effectively on a T-shirt.

The characters, thought bubbles, text and exaggerated poses create a relatively busy composition.

Although I enjoy the energy of the finished image, I'm uncertain whether the same arrangement would translate successfully onto a garment.

The illustration was developed as a piece of artwork, but clothing introduces additional constraints regarding scale, placement and readability.

I haven't yet tested the design physically, so I'm reluctant to make a definitive judgement.

I have the equipment available to experiment with the production process, and I'd still like to see how the illustration behaves when transferred onto fabric.

However, with my current responsibilities, finding the time and energy to pursue that experiment remains a challenge.

Reflections and a New Direction

Regardless of whether the illustration eventually becomes part of the clothing project, I found the process valuable.

It allowed me to explore more exaggerated character expressions, experiment with visual comedy and consider how multiple narrative elements might interact within a single composition.

It also highlighted some limitations of my approach.

In particular, I became increasingly aware of how important the overall shape of an illustration might be when designing for clothing.

Perhaps a more contained composition, organised around a central geometric shape such as a circle, would provide a clearer focal point.

This could help retain the energy and expressiveness of the characters while making the artwork more visually cohesive and easier to position on a garment.

Rather than abandoning the original illustration, I intend to keep the possibility of producing it open.

For my next experiment, however, I decided to pursue this idea of a more circular composition and explore how exaggerated perspective might help organise a complex scene around a stronger central focus.
