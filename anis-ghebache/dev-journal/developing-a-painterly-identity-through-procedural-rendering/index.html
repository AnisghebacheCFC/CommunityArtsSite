Developing a Painterly Identity Through Procedural Rendering

By Anis Ghebache

Developing a Painterly Identity Through Procedural Rendering

Once the tower was functioning, I began considering its artistic direction. I wasn't particularly interested in making the geometry appear realistic or demonstrating the technical capabilities of Three.js for their own sake.

I wanted the tower to feel painted.

This aesthetic is an extension of my previous creative practice, particularly my most recent short film, where I explored a similar visual language. Carrying that approach into an interactive environment felt like a natural continuation of my work.

For me, artistic direction is as much about restraint as it is about experimentation. I would rather create something visually coherent than something technically impressive but disconnected from its surroundings.

Procedural Brushstrokes

I began exploring methods of generating painterly surfaces using shaders.

One approach would have been to create individual brushstroke images and apply them as textures. This would provide a great deal of artistic control over their shape, placement, and appearance.

However, I was interested in the possibility of producing the strokes procedurally.

The current prototype uses a custom shader that generates directional marks across the bricks, varying their shape, thickness, coverage, and orientation. Additional strokes follow the edges, giving the geometry irregular, ink-like outlines.

I also explored painterly post-processing, including the Kuwahara filter, which simplifies areas of colour while retaining some of their boundaries.

These techniques work together to give the surfaces a quality that sits somewhere between three-dimensional geometry and painted illustration.

Painting With Light

One of the aspects I'm most satisfied with is how the brushstrokes interact with lighting.

The shader incorporates the direction of the light into the appearance of the strokes. As the surface changes orientation, one side becomes lighter while another falls into shade.

This resembles the way an illuminated physical object behaves, but photorealism isn't the objective. I want the lighting to reinforce the impression that the bricks have been painted.

The visible strokes and variations in colour are more important to me than creating perfectly smooth surfaces.

I'm particularly interested in how procedural rendering can reproduce some of the irregularity associated with traditional media without relying entirely on pre-existing images.

Artistic Consistency

The purpose of this experiment isn't to make the tower appear more technologically sophisticated.

It's about maintaining a consistent artistic identity across the website.

My plans for the accompanying character involve physical printing, painting, collage, and materials taken from fashion magazines. I want the digital environment to exist alongside those methods rather than appear visually detached from them.

The challenge is to use computational techniques in service of an established artistic direction.

I don't see the procedural approach as a replacement for physical artwork. It's another method of extending that visual language into an interactive space.

What matters to me is that the technology supports the artistic vision, rather than becoming the artistic vision itself.
