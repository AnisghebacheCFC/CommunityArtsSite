Exploring Fisheye Perspective: A Supermarket Illustration for Burlap Friends

By Anis Ghebache

Developing a New Composition

Following my previous experiment with exaggerated cartoon violence, I began considering how a more contained composition might translate effectively onto a T-shirt.

I'd become interested in the possibility of organising an illustration around a central geometric shape, particularly a circle.

The aim was to establish a clearer focal point while preserving the expressive qualities of the Burlap Friends visual language.

This led me to revisit an earlier idea I'd explored while working on my animated short film.

At the time, I'd considered publishing the music I'd composed for Burlap Friends and creating an accompanying album cover.

I wanted to present the characters from a more dynamic perspective than the relatively flat, head-on angles I'd previously used.

Because the characters are visually simple, I felt that changing the perspective could introduce considerable visual interest without requiring me to redesign them.

My original idea involved viewing them from an elevated angle.

For this new illustration, I decided to develop that thinking further by introducing a fisheye perspective.

Why a Supermarket?

I chose a supermarket as the setting because its architecture lends itself particularly well to exaggerated perspective.

The repeating aisles, shelves and intersecting lines create a visually structured environment.

Parallel and perpendicular lines provide strong references for spatial depth, which become particularly interesting when distorted through a fisheye composition.

I wanted the environment to feel expansive and slightly disorientating, despite being an ordinary and familiar location.

The fisheye aesthetic would allow the aisles to curve and converge around the image, creating a sense of depth while supporting the more circular arrangement I was exploring.

I also liked the practical simplicity of the environment.

Although a supermarket contains numerous objects and architectural features, much of that complexity can be represented through repeating shapes and relatively straightforward painting techniques.

This meant I could create an environment that appeared visually elaborate without introducing an unreasonable amount of work.

Placing the Characters Within the Environment

I used visual references to help establish the characters' positioning within the exaggerated perspective.

One of my priorities was ensuring that the characters appeared to inhabit the environment rather than simply being placed on top of it.

The illustration features three child characters, each responding differently to the situation unfolding around them.

The first character looks directly towards the viewer.

This was one of my favourite compositional decisions because it creates an immediate relationship between the character and the audience.

Rather than simply observing the scene, the viewer becomes acknowledged by someone within it.

The second character is peering around the corner of a supermarket aisle.

I wanted this interaction with the environment to help establish the character's physical presence within the space.

By positioning them partially behind an architectural feature, I could reinforce the depth of the composition and make the supermarket feel like a place the characters were genuinely navigating.

The third character appears entirely unbothered.

While the others are responding to the danger around them, this character maintains a deliberately cool and indifferent attitude.

I enjoyed introducing this contrast because it gives the characters distinct personalities without requiring extensive visual explanation.

Their body language alone communicates different attitudes towards the same situation.

Introducing the Monsters

Behind the supermarket aisles, three enormous creatures appear to be searching for the children.

These creatures resemble grotesque, monstrous versions of the characters hiding in the foreground.

This isn't simply an arbitrary aesthetic decision.

Within the mythology of Burlap Friends, the burlap toy characters can become terrifying versions of themselves under the influence of a powerful demon.

I wanted to extend this established supernatural concept into the illustration.

The idea also shares certain qualities with the unsettling imagery associated with The Backrooms, particularly the notion of encountering a distorted and threatening version of something familiar.

By placing these creatures within an otherwise ordinary supermarket, I hoped to create an interesting contrast between the familiar environment and the supernatural danger occupying it.

The large figures also contribute to the exaggerated perspective.

Their imposing scale helps establish a visual hierarchy between the relatively small children in the foreground and the threatening creatures positioned deeper within the environment.

Balancing Horror and Humour

One of the qualities I particularly enjoy about Burlap Friends is the contrast between its simple, expressive character designs and the darker elements of its mythology.

The characters possess an innocent, almost childlike appearance, which makes their encounters with grotesque creatures feel especially strange.

I wanted to maintain that contrast within this illustration.

Although the monsters are threatening, the children's reactions introduce humour and personality.

The character who stares directly at the viewer creates a slightly uncomfortable sense of recognition.

The character peering around the aisle reinforces the feeling of concealment and danger.

Meanwhile, the indifferent character adds a deliberately absurd quality to the scene.

These contrasting reactions create a more interesting composition than if every character were simply expressing fear.

I like the possibility of communicating several different personalities and emotional responses within a single image.

Using Perspective as a Storytelling Tool

What interested me most about this experiment was how perspective could contribute to the narrative.

The fisheye distortion doesn't simply make the illustration more visually dynamic.

It also exaggerates the relationship between the characters, the supermarket and the creatures searching for them.

The curved arrangement of the aisles helps guide the viewer's attention through the environment.

The repeating structural lines establish a sense of depth, while the placement of the characters creates multiple points of interest.

I wanted the viewer to initially appreciate the composition before gradually noticing the more unsettling details.

This sense of discovery was particularly important to me.

Rather than presenting the monsters as the sole focus, I wanted them to emerge through the viewer's exploration of the scene.

The supermarket therefore functions as more than a background.

Its geometry, scale and organisation contribute directly to the atmosphere and storytelling.

Considering the Illustration as a T-Shirt Design

Although I developed the scene as an illustration in its own right, the wider objective remained connected to my proposed clothing project.

Following the challenges I'd encountered with my previous composition, I wanted to experiment with a more cohesive overall shape.

The fisheye approach offered an interesting possibility because its characteristic distortion naturally encourages the eye to move around a central point.

I felt this could lend itself to a more contained graphic suitable for placement on a garment.

At the same time, the supermarket setting allowed me to retain the visual complexity and narrative detail that I enjoy incorporating into my illustrations.

The challenge was finding a balance between these qualities.

An image that works successfully on a screen may become difficult to interpret when reproduced at a smaller scale or viewed on moving fabric.

I therefore wanted to consider the overall silhouette and visual hierarchy of the composition alongside its individual details.

The physical translation remains something I need to test before drawing conclusions about the design's suitability.

Reflections on the Experiment

This experiment allowed me to revisit the visual language of Burlap Friends from a different perspective.

Rather than developing new character designs, I focused on using composition, environmental geometry and exaggerated perspective to create visual interest.

I particularly enjoyed the relationship between the simple characters and the more elaborate supermarket environment.

The fisheye distortion gave the scene a dynamic quality while providing a way to organise its different elements around a more cohesive visual structure.

I was also pleased to find an opportunity to incorporate the film's existing mythology, extending the supernatural relationship between the characters and their monstrous counterparts into a new setting.

The illustration represents another step towards developing a broader visual identity that can exist beyond the original animated film.

For now, I'm interested in continuing to experiment with how these illustrations might translate into handmade clothing.

My longer-term ambition is to combine the expressive character designs I've developed through animation with a production technique that celebrates the physical qualities of handmade work.

I don't yet know exactly what form the finished garments will take, but I'm enjoying the opportunity to explore these ideas without committing prematurely to a single outcome.
