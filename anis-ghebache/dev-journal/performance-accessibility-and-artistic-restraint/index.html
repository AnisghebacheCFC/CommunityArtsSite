Performance, Accessibility and Artistic Restraint

By Anis Ghebache

While developing the tower, I've been thinking about the relationship between visual quality, performance, and accessibility.

My priority is to reduce friction when people access the website. I don't want visitors to need expensive or particularly powerful hardware simply to interact with something I've created.

This has influenced how I'm thinking about rendering performance.

Testing the Prototype

I tested the current implementation on a Samsung Galaxy M53, where it was running at approximately 40 frames per second, and I consider that an acceptable result for the type of experience I'm creating.

The website isn't a competitive game, and its interactions don't require exceptionally high frame rates. The purpose is to explore an environment, contribute to a collective artwork, and engage with its visual elements.

I've therefore been considering limiting the rendering rate to approximately 24 frames per second.

I don't regard 24 FPS as a technical limitation

Frame Rate as an Artistic Decision

A lower frame rate may also complement the painterly rendering style.

I've noticed occasional jittering associated with the paint effect. Rather than automatically pursuing higher frame rates, I'm interested in whether a more restrained update frequency could produce movement that feels appropriate to the environment.

There is a distinction between animation that is technically smooth and animation that is artistically suitable.

My intention isn't to make the experience feel unresponsive. It's to establish a level of motion that supports the aesthetic without consuming unnecessary computational resources.

Of course, limiting the frame rate doesn't automatically make the rendering inexpensive. The cost of individual frames, shader calculations, memory allocation, and the number of objects being rendered still matters.

These are aspects I want to continue measuring.

Designing for Accessibility

The broader motivation is accessibility.

Community Arts is intended to encourage participation, so it would be contradictory to create a digital environment that unnecessarily excludes people because of their hardware.

I want to design for devices with relatively limited capabilities rather than assume everyone will be accessing the website through a powerful computer.

This means benchmarking the prototype, testing it on different devices, and evaluating how the rendering techniques scale as the number of bricks increases.

The procedural paint effects are worth retaining because they contribute substantially to the artistic identity, but I don't want to maximise resource consumption simply because the technology allows it.

For me, again, restraint is part of artistic direction.

A technically ambitious project doesn't necessarily need to demand the maximum performance available. The more interesting objective is determining how little computation is required to achieve the experience I actually want.

I want the work to remain visually distinctive, accessible, and sustainable as the project develops!
