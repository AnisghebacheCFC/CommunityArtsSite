What Happens When You Touch a Brick?

By Anis Ghebache

When I first started working on the tower, I was mainly thinking about what happens when somebody adds a brick. I wanted them to see where it went and be able to find it again later.

I've started thinking more about what happens when somebody arrives at the tower for the first time. The structure is already there. It already contains contributions from people they might never have met. What can they do with it?

At the moment, turning the tower lets you look around it. I'd like the individual bricks to give you a reason to stop turning.

Moving a Brick Out of Place

The interaction I'm working towards begins when somebody selects a brick. I want it to move outwards from the tower as if something behind it has gently pushed it free.

Once it has cleared the surrounding bricks, it can float forward into its own space. You should be able to see where it came from and follow its movement without having to think about what the interface is doing.

The first part of that movement matters to me. A brick is usually something you expect to stay put. If it moves just a little while it's still surrounded by the other bricks, you can see that it's the same piece you were looking at a moment ago.

Spending Time With One Contribution

There are two scales to the tower. From a distance, you see the structure that everybody has helped build. Up close, you can spend a moment with something one person chose to leave behind.

I want the floating brick to make that change of scale feel natural. It gives you a little space to look at the individual contribution before you return to looking at the whole tower.

I've thought about putting the brick to one side once it comes forward. That would leave some of the tower in view, which I think is important. Even when you're looking at one contribution, you can still see the larger thing it belongs to.

Returning the Brick

When somebody closes the view, I'd like the floating brick to dissolve and reappear in its original position.

I considered having it fly all the way back. I prefer the idea of a brief fade because the visitor has already seen where it came from. By that point, they're probably ready to turn the tower again or carry on down the page.

There's also something I like about the brick being back exactly where you found it. Somebody else can come along, find it in the same place and have their own moment with it.


A phrase I kept coming back to while thinking about this was “touching generosity.”

The tower began as a way to give people's goodwill a physical form. Making a brick move when you touch it takes that idea a little further. A person's contribution can do something in the hands of somebody who comes along later.

I want there to be a sense of play in that. You can spend a minute turning the tower and seeing what people have left there. You can do that on your first visit, even if you haven't contributed a brick of your own or been to one of our workshops.

Leaving the Tower Open

Community projects can look quite established from the outside. You see names and photographs and people who already seem to know each other. It can be difficult to tell where you fit into that as somebody who's just arrived.

I think the tower can give people a small way in. You can see that other people have been here before you and that what they left is there to be explored. There’s also room for you to come back and leave something of your own.

The next thing I want to get right is the movement itself. I'd like to see how far the brick needs to push out before you can feel it come free, and how slowly it can float forward before the interaction starts to feel like something you're waiting for.
