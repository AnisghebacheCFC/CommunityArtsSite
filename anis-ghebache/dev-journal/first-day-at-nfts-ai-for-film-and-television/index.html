First Day at NFTS: AI for Film and Television

By Anis Ghebache

My first day on the National Film and Television School's AI for Film and TV programme took place at Adobe's London offices, in a building rather amusingly named the White Collar Factory.

I couldn't help finding the name ironic, particularly given Adobe's reputation surrounding subscription contracts and the wider conversations about creative labour and automation. There was something strangely fitting about beginning an AI filmmaking course in a building with that name.

Introductions and AI Fundamentals

We began with introductions and an overview of the six-month programme, followed by a presentation from Angie French exploring generative AI, large language models, multimodal systems and agentic AI.

Much of the material reinforced concepts I was already familiar with, although it was interesting to see how these technologies were introduced to people from different creative backgrounds and disciplines.

A Drawing of a Sailor

During the project-setting session, I began thinking about developing my short film idea, A Drawing of a Sailor.

The story follows a child who draws a sailor. After the child's father dies, his spirit possesses the drawing.

It's an idea I've been interested in exploring, and I'm curious to see how AI might help bring it to life while preserving my own artistic direction and creative intentions.

Adobe Firefly and First Impressions

We finished the day with a demonstration of Adobe Firefly and its applications in creative production.

Personally, I would have preferred this practical session earlier in the day, while everyone was still fresh. By the afternoon, I was feeling rather tired, and others seemed to be experiencing the same thing. That said, I appreciate that scheduling and presenter availability may have influenced the order of events.

Looking Ahead

Overall, it was an interesting introduction to the programme and an opportunity to meet people approaching AI from different areas of the film and television industry.

I'm particularly looking forward to moving beyond the fundamentals and experimenting with these technologies in practice.

For me, the most exciting aspect isn't necessarily what AI can generate independently, but how it can be incorporated into a deliberate and personal creative process.

Over the coming six months, I'll be documenting those experiments, discoveries and reflections here.
