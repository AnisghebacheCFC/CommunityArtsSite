From Backdrop to Collective Architecture

By Anis Ghebache

The original idea for my website was relatively simple. I wanted to create an interactive environment in which a character could walk across the screen, accompanied by a backdrop that changed periodically. I was considering featuring a different artwork each month, allowing the website to evolve visually without necessarily changing its underlying structure.

The idea of a tower came later, almost spontaneously. I began thinking about what I could do with the environment, which led me to a tower, then to bricks, and eventually to the possibility of allowing visitors to contribute to its construction.

I'd already explored the symbolism of the tower in an earlier journal entry. What interests me now is how that idea has developed into a functioning prototype.

Developing the Structure

Using Three.js, I've been experimenting with a cylindrical structure composed of individual bricks. Each brick occupies a calculated position around the circumference, with subsequent rows stacking vertically as contributions accumulate.

The geometry itself is relatively straightforward. What makes the structure interesting to me is the relationship between its visual representation and its intended purpose.

The tower isn't supposed to be a monument that I construct independently. Its growth is determined by participation, with each brick representing an individual contribution.

I've also been considering the longevity of the structure. Rather than creating something that looks impressive immediately, I want an environment capable of accommodating growth over an extended period.

This influenced decisions about how bricks are positioned, how contributors can find their individual bricks, and how the structure can remain navigable as it becomes larger.

An Environment Rather Than an Object

Seeing the tower rendered didn't fundamentally change my understanding of its symbolism. Instead, it encouraged me to consider how the structure would fit into the wider aesthetic of the website.

The tower is only one component of a larger interactive environment. I've also been developing ideas for a two-dimensional character, constructed through physical artwork and animation, who will inhabit the same world.

I want these elements to feel connected rather than assembled independently.

My attention has therefore shifted from establishing the structure to developing its visual language, interactivity, and relationship with the other elements of the website.

The underlying concept remains the same: an environment that grows through the contributions of others, rather than simply presenting my own work.
