Catching Up with Louise

By Anis Ghebache

A Brief Introduction

On Tuesday 27 October, I returned to London College of Communication to meet Louise, who coordinates the Postgraduate Peer Mentorship Programme.

Having missed the morning portion of our initial training session due to my commitments at Draper Hall, Louise had kindly offered to meet with me individually to go through the material I'd missed.

We arranged to meet at the Academic Support Office in the library's Learning Zone.

The interaction was relatively brief, consisting primarily of a conversation between Louise and me.

There wasn't a great deal to report in terms of activities, but I appreciated the opportunity to clarify the responsibilities of the role and learn a little more about the programme.

The Development of the Programme

One thing that interested me was hearing how the mentorship programme had developed.

Louise explained that it had begun as a relatively small initiative before gradually expanding across different courses.

I found this encouraging, particularly given my own efforts to develop Community Arts.

There's something reassuring about learning that an established programme began on a modest scale.

I often think about how initiatives can grow organically through consistency and the relationships they establish, rather than requiring everything to be fully developed from the outset.

Although Community Arts and the Peer Mentorship Programme operate in different contexts, I see similarities in their underlying purpose: bringing people together and creating opportunities for mutual support.

Looking Ahead

With the introductory training behind me, the next step would be to meet some of the students participating in the programme.

I'm particularly interested in discovering what students hope to achieve and whether my experiences can offer them a useful perspective.

As I reflected on in my first entry, I'm hoping that mentorship will provide some structure for my naturally spontaneous inclination towards helping others.

I don't necessarily want to approach these relationships with a predetermined idea of what somebody should become.

Instead, I'd like to offer an opportunity for conversation, encouragement and the exchange of ideas.

For now, I'm curious to see how the programme develops.

A Small Observation

Earlier that morning, I was out walking and found myself admiring the fallen leaves scattered across the pavement.

We're entering that period between autumn and winter when leaves have begun collecting on the ground but haven't yet completely dried out.

I was enjoying their variety of shapes, colours and textures, wondering how I might incorporate some of these qualities into my artistic practice.

Amongst them, I noticed a discarded doormat label bearing the words "This comfortably me feel so good" beneath an enormous "DOOR MAT".

The wonderfully awkward English made me laugh, and I found the whole thing rather endearing.

I took a photograph simply because it made me smile.

Nothing particularly profound, just one of those unexpected little observations that makes an ordinary morning more enjoyable.
