Void: A Creative Collaboration Begins

By Anis Ghebache

Through my work with Community Arts at Draper Hall, I recently connected with Liza, the writer and director of a short film titled Void. On Wednesday, we spent much of the afternoon discussing her vision for the project and exploring how I could contribute through editing, visual effects and creative direction.

While Anna facilitated the art workshop, I helped supply materials and sat with Liza to discuss the film's visual language.

A Hauntingly Beautiful Absurdity

Void has a hauntingly beautiful quality, with an underlying sense of existential absurdism. Much of our conversation revolved around how the environment could reinforce that atmosphere.

I arrived with an edited concept image proposing the removal of the brick wall behind the characters, replacing it with an expansive, seemingly infinite space. Liza responded enthusiastically, and the idea opened up further possibilities for introducing symbolic objects and creating tension.

One suggestion involved a boiler suit suspended in the background, gently swaying in an imaginary breeze. The film features three central characters dressed in boiler suits, alongside an unseen fourth presence that stalks them from behind a pillar. I felt that an empty, moving suit could subtly evoke that presence without revealing anything.

We also discussed the ceiling pipes. I proposed simplifying their arrangement to emphasise the number three, reflecting the three central characters and their interactions.

Finding a Shared Creative Language

One of the most enjoyable aspects of our conversation was discovering how similarly we approached symbolism and storytelling.

Liza jokingly mentioned that she'd been warned editors might want to add things and change her film. To her surprise, she found herself agreeing with many of my suggestions.

Naturally, I had no shortage of ideas, and thankfully she was equally comfortable pushing back when necessary.

We discussed the pacing of the performances, particularly how fatigue during filming had sometimes caused the actors to rush their dialogue. I suggested exploring editing techniques, potentially including generative AI, to extend certain pauses and allow moments of silence to breathe.

Given the film's existential atmosphere, I felt that these moments could be just as expressive as the dialogue itself.

The Poster and the Soul of the Film

I also brought along an initial poster concept that emphasised the chair and door, two elements I felt were central to the film's atmosphere.

Liza, however, preferred a close-up of a character sitting on the chair and turning around. She explained that the character's expression carried the meaning of the film.

The shadows beneath the woman's brow partially obscure her face, creating a sense of absence and ambiguity that she felt captured the spirit of Void.

Although I still appreciated my environmental approach, I understood her perspective. I think being able to disagree constructively is an important part of creative collaboration, particularly when both people are working towards the same vision.

Working Towards the Premiere

With the premiere approaching in approximately two weeks, we also had to be realistic about our ambitions.

Liza encouraged me to prioritise assembling a rough cut and establishing the pacing before introducing additional visual effects and experimental elements.

It's a sensible approach, especially when the possibilities for creative experimentation can feel almost endless.

I left the meeting feeling excited about the collaboration, the opportunities to develop my editing and emerging AI skills, and the creative conversations still to come.

Ultimately, my intention isn't to transform Void into something entirely different. It's to understand Liza's vision and help make her dream tangible.
