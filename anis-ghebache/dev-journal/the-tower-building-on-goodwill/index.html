The Tower: Building on Goodwill

By Anis Ghebache

The Idea

I've been thinking about introducing a rotating brick tower to the Community Arts website. A structure that grows with every person who chooses to support what we're doing.

The idea is relatively simple. Anyone visiting the website can contribute a brick to the tower, free of charge. Each brick is placed beside the previous one, gradually forming a circular structure. Once a layer is complete, another begins above it.

Over time, the tower grows taller, becoming a visible record of the people who have supported Community Arts.

I want it to feel like something physical, despite existing entirely online. Something people can watch grow, interact with and return to.

Building on Goodwill

Community Arts exists because of the goodwill of people who believe in what we're trying to achieve.

We've been fortunate to receive support from individuals, artists, local organisations and people willing to give their time without expecting anything in return.

I don't want to lose sight of that as the organisation grows.

Each brick represents a person who has chosen to support us, however small that gesture might be. There doesn't need to be a financial contribution or any particular commitment. Simply taking the time to participate is enough.

Each brick of goodwill sets the foundation we will build our community on.

I think there's something meaningful about representing that support through a structure. A tower cannot stand without its foundations, and Community Arts cannot grow without the people who make its work possible.

Equality Without Uniformity

I've been considering how individual contributions should appear within the tower.

Most bricks will have slightly different shades of red, orange and yellow, reflecting the natural variations found in brickwork. I don't want the structure to look entirely uniform.

I'd also like to introduce distinctive bricks for people affiliated with universities, businesses and other organisations.

For example, someone associated with UAL might have a black-and-white patterned brick, while someone from Walworth Garden might have a green-and-white design.

These would be visual references to their respective organisations, rather than simply attaching logos to the surface.

Importantly, every brick would occupy the same amount of space.

Whether someone is an individual supporter, a student, or a representative of a large institution, their contribution is structurally equal.

There may eventually be a distinction between someone declaring an affiliation and an organisation formally supporting us, but I don't think that needs to translate into a hierarchy of size or prominence.

A Tower That Remembers

I want contributors to be able to find their bricks again.

Each contribution would receive a unique identifier that could be searched on the website. Entering that identifier would rotate the tower towards the relevant brick, allowing someone to revisit their contribution or share it with somebody else.

Clicking a brick could reveal the contributor's name, their affiliation and perhaps a short message, should they wish to leave one.

Anonymity would also be possible.

I particularly like the idea of somebody returning months or years later and finding the same brick, now surrounded by hundreds or thousands of others.

Their contribution hasn't changed, but the structure around it has.

Making the Experience Immediate

One of my priorities is making the interaction feel responsive.

When somebody contributes a brick, I want them to see it join the tower immediately. There shouldn't be a long wait before they can experience what they've contributed.

However, contributions would still need to be reviewed before becoming publicly visible.

My current idea is to show the submitted brick immediately to its contributor, then permanently add it to the public structure once approved.

This would allow the experience to remain immediate without compromising the integrity of the tower.

I'd also like to retain some administrative control over individual bricks, particularly when recognising organisations that have formally supported Community Arts.

A Structure That Grows With Us

I haven't decided how tall the tower will eventually become, or what its practical limits might be.

If it grows too large, I could archive the existing structure and begin another, while keeping previous towers available to explore.

For now, I'm more interested in seeing how people respond to it.

There's something appealing about creating a structure whose appearance I can't entirely predict. I can establish its form, materials and rules, but its growth depends on the people who choose to participate.

That's also how I see Community Arts developing.

I can establish the organisation, organise workshops and create opportunities, but its future depends on the relationships we build and the people who believe in what we're doing.

I want the tower to reflect that relationship.

Not as a monument to the organisation itself, but as a record of the people who have helped it grow.

And hopefully, as the years go by, there will be quite a lot of bricks.


Please accept my crude blender mockup of what I want to create.
