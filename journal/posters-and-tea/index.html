17 August 2026

Posters and tea

I designed the posters and printed them at the local library, which turned out to be quite expensive. Liza kindly offered to help with printing the occasional poster at the community centre after that. I haven’t taken her up on it yet, but it’s good to know I don’t have to spend quite so much every time.

For the design, I took some elements from the existing Food Hub and Bingo posters, including the joined-up handwriting style. I wanted what I was doing to complement what was already there. The sessions already had their own identity, and I was conscious of coming into that space with something new. I wanted the workshops to add something for the people attending.

I used block lettering and layered ink textures over it to make it look as though it had been printed by hand. I was quite happy with how the posters came out. They did what I wanted them to do.

Because I only had a limited number, we handed them out to each table and asked for them back afterwards so we could use them again. They were doing a few rounds.

Around this time, I was also speaking with Bird & Blend about a possible collaboration. Miki had heard about them through a friend, who mentioned that they were open to working with small community-led projects.

I liked the idea of having different teas for people to try during the sessions. There’s something comforting about having a cup of tea while you sit and make something, and Bird & Blend had so many flavours that people might find something they wouldn’t usually choose.

That was something I wanted for Community Arts too. Somewhere people could feel comfortable enough to try something unfamiliar, whether that was making a print or just choosing a different cup of tea.
