Red cabbage and changing colour

Following our experiments with onion skins and turmeric, I brought red cabbage into the next session. Grating it into a cup and leaving it with a little water produced a deep purple liquid. Adding lemon juice shifted it towards red, while bicarbonate of soda turned it blue. The cabbage’s anthocyanin pigments respond to changes in acidity, allowing one extract to produce several colours.

I demonstrated the changes to the group, and the response was enthusiastic. Watching the colour transform added an element of discovery to the activity. It made me think about how we could develop this into an interactive workshop for children, while the interest from our older participants showed how much appeal the experiment already had.

I also tried cornflour as a thickener. It helped the colour sit on the paper, but introduced some clumps and made the mixture cloudy, reducing the vividness that had first attracted me to it. There is still a balance to find between colour strength and consistency.

Alongside collage, we focused more on calligraphy and making marks. We began with paintbrushes, but the stirring sticks produced some particularly interesting results. Something we’d used to prepare the colour became another tool for working with it.

Printing was less successful. The mixtures were still too fluid to produce the impressions I wanted, even with the thickener. That gave me a clearer distinction to explore: a colour that works beautifully for drawing or lettering may need a different formulation for printing.

This session opened up more possibilities than it resolved. I’m interested in continuing the experiments, both to improve how the colours behave and to build an activity around the changes themselves.
