The first session

For our first session, we kept things small and focused on botanical printing. We rolled paint onto leaves, placed paper over them and used a clean roller on the back to apply pressure. Peeling the paper away revealed the patterns and textures transferred from the plants.

The format worked well alongside the usual afternoon activities. People could join us, try a print and then return to their word search or crossword. I liked that participation could be brief. There was room to experiment without having to commit to a whole session.

We also had a variety of teas and plant material from a few different sources. During renovations, the Barbican Conservatory had been making cuttings available for people to propagate. Following a meeting with them, I was able to secure some dried plant material for our workshops. I was proud to acknowledge their contribution on the poster. A local plant shop also kindly sold us fern and monstera leaves to use.

It was a good start. Keeping the first session manageable gave me a chance to see how the activity fitted into the afternoon and how people chose to engage. I was also beginning to think more practically about costs and what we would need to sustain the workshops as they developed.

Lunch was graciously provided by the hall, roasted chicken and veg!
