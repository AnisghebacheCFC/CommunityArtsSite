Onion skins, turmeric and accidental dinosaurs

I’d been thinking about natural dyes for several sessions, so this week we began experimenting with onion skins and turmeric. We prepared the colours at the community centre, using the boiling water available for tea, stirring the ingredients and adjusting the amount of water as we went.

Getting the ratio right was a challenge. The onion skins produced a light brown colour, but the liquid tended to pool on the watercolour paper rather than soak in. The turmeric gave us a yellow-brown colour that spread into the paper more readily, although it left a visible graininess. These were early experiments, and the differences gave us something useful to work from.

We used refillable water brushes, with squeezable reservoirs in their handles, to apply the colours. Participants made their own splotches, then used pencils to draw into and around them, finding shapes suggested by the marks. Dinosaurs kept appearing. I’m not sure why they were such a recurring theme, but it became an amusing part of the afternoon.

I enjoyed how the unpredictability of the colour became part of the activity. A mark didn’t need to resemble anything when it landed on the page. People could turn the paper, look again and decide what they wanted to make of it.

The experiments also brought our materials into closer focus. I want to reduce the waste we produce and explore processes whose leftovers could safely be composted. If we eventually offer activities for younger children, I’d also like to investigate materials appropriate for that setting, assessing the finished mixtures rather than assuming that natural ingredients are automatically safe.

There is still plenty to test, including colour concentration and whether a thickener or binder would give us more control. For this session, though, the imperfect results gave people enough to begin imagining.
