Boston fern: finding strength in simple forms

For this session, we worked with Boston fern (Nephrolepis exaltata), making simple monoprints with one ink colour per print. Its arching fronds are divided into small leaflets along a central stem, giving them a repeating, almost ladder-like structure.

That structure translated beautifully onto paper. Using a single colour kept the impressions vivid and strong, allowing the rhythm and symmetry of the fronds to hold our attention. I particularly enjoyed how much detail emerged from such a straightforward process. There was little need to complicate it.

We continued our new tradition of giving the plant to a participant to take home. I liked that the plant could remain part of someone’s everyday life after providing material for the session.

The discarded foliage left me less satisfied. Once coated in our printing ink, it went into the general waste bin. Working with plants had encouraged us to look more closely at nature, but I was becoming increasingly conscious of what our process left behind.

This prompted me to consider natural dyes and to investigate materials that might allow us to compost the used foliage safely. It was a question to carry into future sessions: how could we preserve the pleasure and visual strength of these prints while being more thoughtful about what we used?
