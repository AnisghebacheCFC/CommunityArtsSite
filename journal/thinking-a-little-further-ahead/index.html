Thinking a little further ahead

Around the time I began developing Community Arts, I noticed a poster for children’s creative play sessions in my local Sainsbury’s. I photographed it. Once I’d started planning workshops myself, I found I was paying closer attention to these notices, wondering how the sessions were run and whether they managed to reach the people they were intended for.

This one reminded me of attending baby group as a toddler. I remember the play area, running around and singing songs. I also remember my mother helping and joining in with activities. Looking back, her involvement interests me as much as the activities themselves.

My initial thinking for Community Arts had focused on older residents and opportunities for connection. But that memory raised a wider question about who could participate, and in what capacity. A parent could attend with their child, contribute to an activity and leave with ideas they could use at home. Someone without an arts background could discover an interest in helping others learn. I’d like the organisation to make room for that, alongside opportunities for artists and aspiring workshop facilitators to develop their practice.

There is also something I want to explore in bringing different generations together through making. Botanical printing seems particularly suited to this. It involves working with available materials, and its abstract qualities leave room for unexpected results. Age and experience wouldn’t necessarily determine whose work attracts interest. A child and an older participant might recognise something in each other’s prints, exchange an idea or become curious about how the other approached it.

That possibility matters to me. Working alongside other people gives us opportunities to experiment socially as well as creatively: to offer something, respond to someone else and find a way to contribute.

Designing an activity that holds the interest of different age groups would require care. A shared table alone wouldn’t guarantee a shared experience. But I want Community Arts to remain open to exploring this, with activities that give people a reason to engage with one another and enough room to discover what they can contribute.
