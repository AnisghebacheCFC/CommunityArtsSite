Finding a richer colour

Black walnut hull gave us a rich brown unlike anything we’d produced so far. I’d tried tea previously, but this had a depth closer to coffee or dark oak. The dried hull was crumbly enough to break into small particles, and stirring it into hot water released a beautiful colour.

The mixture felt slightly thicker than our earlier experiments and worked more successfully on paper. Lolly sticks produced particularly strong marks, beginning with concentrated colour that gradually trailed away as the liquid ran out. Brushes gave us broader strokes, but the colour felt less concentrated. We explored drawing and calligraphy, enjoying the differences between the tools.

During the session, I went to the local art shop to buy gum arabic and began experimenting with it in the mixture. I wanted to improve how the colour handled and settled on the paper. Developing something reliable will take repeated adjustments, but the walnut felt like a promising starting point.

I left the hull particles in the paper cups so we could add more hot water and continue using them. They mostly stayed at the bottom without interfering with the brushes or sticks. One participant took a different approach, using her finger to work the particles onto the paper and create a scratchy texture. Something I’d kept to avoid waste became a material to draw with in its own right.

We also continued with collage. Anna has a good eye for selecting images and a way of encouraging people to join in. Having several activities available gives participants more choice, though I’m conscious of keeping the table approachable rather than overwhelming. After a few weeks together, people are becoming familiar with what they enjoy and what they want to try.

These sessions are helping us develop both the materials and the way we introduce them. Each experiment gives us something to adjust, while participants keep finding possibilities I hadn’t planned for.
