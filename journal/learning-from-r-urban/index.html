Learning from R-Urban

Miki and I spent a few hours volunteering at R-Urban in Poplar. She had been before, but this was my first visit. We watered plants, did some trimming and helped with the general upkeep. We also broke down cardboard for composting, carefully removing tape and any other plastic.

What stayed with me was how readily they welcomed people whose involvement might be occasional. We both get busy, and couldn’t necessarily promise to come regularly. They seemed accustomed to that. Tasks were explained clearly, and there was room to contribute for the time we had.

People were happy to work together, including crouching down to help fill watering cans. After several hours working in the rain, we sat down to homegrown tomatoes and other vegetables. The food was excellent, and everyone seemed happy to stay, eat and talk. Having worked alongside one another gave us something to share by the time we reached the table.

I have some experience in landscaping and gardening, but this visit deepened my appreciation for plant life. Seeing the care taken over what was grown, reused and returned to the compost made me think more closely about the materials we use in our own workshops.

It left me wanting to explore alternatives to acrylic paint and understand which materials could safely be composted after use. We were already using plants to make prints. I wanted to consider what happened to those materials afterwards, and whether our activities could eventually produce waste that helped nourish the surrounding plant life.
