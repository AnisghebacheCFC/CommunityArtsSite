A plant in return

This week, we experimented with crushed blueberries. They produced scratchy, splotchy marks on the paper, and we kept the activity abstract, playing with the material rather than trying to make it behave like a conventional paint or printing ink.

It was a relaxed afternoon. I spent time moving between tables, chatting with people and joining in with crosswords. I enjoy these opportunities to catch up. As we become more familiar with one another, the conversations are becoming as much a part of the sessions as the art.

One of the women brought me an aloe vera plant. She had intended to give me something to propagate, but decided to give me the whole plant instead, as a thank-you for the plants I’d given her previously. It was an unexpected and very sweet gesture. I’d offered those plants as gifts for taking part and making art with us, so receiving one in return meant a lot.

I’m now looking after it and wondering whether its gel might have a place in our material experiments. I haven’t tested it as a thickener yet. Gum arabic has been useful, but I’m still trying to achieve more body without losing the strength of the colours. A binder and a thickener serve different purposes, and understanding that distinction is becoming part of the learning process.

For now, the aloe is a plant to care for. Its possible uses can wait. What stayed with me from this session was the thoughtfulness of someone bringing something in for me.
