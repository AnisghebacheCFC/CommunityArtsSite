Starting Community Arts

I went to Draper Hall when I was very young, before the age of ten, for activities and arts workshops. I remember being surprised that my building was included in the community there, alongside Draper House and the surrounding flats. I didn’t really know that something like that existed for us. As a kid, it was comforting to feel part of it.

I’ve lived in Elephant & Castle my whole life, and the hall has always felt like an anchor for people in the area. Some of my neighbours have been living here longer than I’ve been alive. They’ve lost people along the way, and I know that some feel disconnected. I wanted to create an opportunity for people to get to know each other and have something to look forward to.

I also knew what it was like to be an art student struggling to find opportunities that felt fulfilling. I thought there could be something in bringing those things together, giving students and emerging artists a chance to share their skills while making something with people in the area.

I emailed Draper Together, and the director and staff were kind enough to have a chat with me about the idea. It was a very sunny day. I remember having to move into the shade to carry on talking, while people were lining up outside for the food bank.

We spoke about my work at UAL and what I’d learned from working with students. I’ve always liked asking international students about their experiences because they’re so different from mine. I’ve lived here all my life, whereas some of them are trying to find their place somewhere completely unfamiliar.

One of my worries was whether enough people would come. The staff suggested bringing the workshops into their existing Wednesday hot food and bingo club, which gave me a way to start with people who were already coming to the hall.

I’d recently completed a workshop at Walworth Living Room, and at this point I was developing the visual identity, flyers and posters. I didn’t know how the first session would go or how interested people would be, but I had somewhere to begin.

It was nice to be back in a place I’d used as a kid, this time with the chance to contribute something myself.
