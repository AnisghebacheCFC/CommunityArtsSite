Asparagus Fern

Our visits to Societree, the local plant shop, have become a useful part of preparing for the workshops. Whenever I go in, we have a quick conversation about different plants, what might work for printing and what they would recommend.

This week, I chose an asparagus fern from their weekly offers. Its foliage is fine, dense and feathery, quite different from the ferns we’d used previously. They hadn’t initially recommended it for printing, but I wanted to introduce some variety and see what we could do with it.

Their reservations made sense once we started. The fine foliage offered very little surface for transferring paint, and pressing it between sheets of paper didn’t produce the definition we’d achieved with broader leaves. We ended up with accidental negative impressions, though these were less interesting than I’d hoped.

Using the plant as a brush proved more promising. Dragging it across the paper produced scratchy marks, giving us another way to work with its texture. It was a useful reminder that choosing unfamiliar materials means allowing the activity to change in response to what they actually do.

One resident made a piece with bold blues that particularly caught our attention. We spontaneously decided to give them the plant to take home, and they were delighted. It’s something I’d like to continue. Several participants are interested in plants, and taking one home could extend that interest beyond the session. I hope this one settles into its new home and lives a long life.
