Collage, texture and the senses

This session gave us more room to experiment with collage. We cut images and lettering from a Tesco magazine, combining photographs of food and bold typography with plant material. Some leaves were used for printing, while others became part of the collages themselves. It was enjoyable to see these familiar materials brought into unexpected combinations.

Miki brought a dried material with a delicate, branching texture and a distinctive scent. I’m not sure what it was, but it worked beautifully in the collages. She was also testing sensory elements for an upcoming workshop, exploring what different materials could offer through touch and smell as well as appearance.

That interested me because it widened my thinking about what an activity could involve. Choosing, handling and smelling a material could be as engaging as deciding where to place it. It also brought me back to the possibility of working with younger children, and how we might create opportunities for them to explore through their senses.

The plant I brought was a prayer plant, Maranta leuconeura. Its patterned leaves were beautiful, and we considered their potential for pressing. But I found myself increasingly reluctant to remove them. As much as I enjoy working with plants, I’m beginning to question when using a leaf adds something worthwhile and when I would rather leave it growing. I’d like to explore ways of working with their forms and patterns while keeping more of the plant intact.

Dave also produced several drawings, which I’m looking forward to sharing here. His quick, assured approach has become a familiar part of the sessions, alongside the slower process of arranging and rearranging collage.

We finished with bangers and mash and time to enjoy the food together. Between the printing, drawing, collage and Miki’s experiments, there were several ways into the afternoon. That variety is something I want to keep developing.
