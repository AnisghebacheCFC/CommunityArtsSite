From Seeds to Autumn Leaves

This week, we brought in a selection of seeds, including chilli and coriander, which prompted a discussion about growing conditions, germination and the best times of year to begin planting. One participant recalled growing some of these plants in her own garden, sharing her experience as we compared the different varieties.

I've also been paying more attention to the fallen leaves accumulating around the neighbourhood. There is an enormous variety of shapes, sizes and textures, and the transition into autumn presents an interesting opportunity to revisit our earlier botanical printing experiments.

Until now, we've primarily worked with fresh leaves, whose flexible surfaces lend themselves well to printing. Dried leaves introduce a different set of possibilities. I expect their brittle structure to produce more fragmented, scratchy impressions, with irregular patches of colour where the surface makes less contact with the paper. I'm particularly interested in preserving these imperfections rather than trying to reproduce the cleaner impressions we've achieved previously.

For an upcoming session, I'd like to combine these textures with collage, perhaps introducing a small spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum) as a contrasting subject. Its long, narrow leaves could provide an interesting visual counterpoint to the broader, irregular shapes of fallen autumn foliage. Combining cut-paper compositions with botanical prints could also bring some of our earlier techniques together.

Our experiments with natural dyes and handmade paints have taken a temporary back seat, although there are still several approaches I'd like to develop. Returning to conventional poster paints for printing would allow us to focus more closely on the physical characteristics of the leaves themselves.

All three facilitators were present this Wednesday. I also spent part of the morning discussing the editing and visual direction of a locally produced short film with Liza. It's encouraging to see how the creative relationships developing at Draper Hall are beginning to extend into other artistic collaborations.
